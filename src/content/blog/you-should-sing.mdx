---
title: "You Don’t Have to Be Good to Sing"
date: "2026-11-01"
excerpt: "People apologize to me for their singing all the time. I wish they wouldn’t."
---

Being known as a singer has a strange side effect, which is that people apologize to me for their
voices. They’ll stop humming when I walk in. It makes me sad every time, because I think singing is one
of the best things a person can do, and at some point we decided it only counts if you’re good enough
to get paid for it.

The comparison doesn’t even make sense. The pop stars people measure themselves against have elite
coaches, medical care most of us can’t afford, and a whole career’s worth of daily practice. You’ve got
a car and a song you like. Nobody’s asking you to release an album or get up at karaoke. I’d skip
belting it in the supermarket, but your friends can handle hearing you in the passenger seat, and if
they can’t, that’s useful information about your friends.

A lot of my own training was just never stopping. I sang with the radio and with my brother, and I
still catch myself singing in public, because there’s nearly always a song going in my head. Choir and
lessons helped, but all those hours helped as much. People who stop themselves all the time miss out on
those hours.

You could also be wrong about what your voice even is. I told people for years that I was a
bass-baritone, mostly because my upper range scared me and I was afraid of cracking. The director of On
the Rocks told me at my audition that I was a tenor, and I went on to sing the highest parts in the
group. Getting there had more to do with finding what felt easy than with getting anything technically
right.

So sing something today, badly if you need to. If you’d like company, my
[voice lessons](/lessons/voice) can start with sirens and a lot of being silly.
