If you've ever booked studio time or shopped for someone to work on your track, you've probably
seen "mixing" and "mastering" listed side by side and wondered whether you need one, the other, or
both. They're related, but they're genuinely different jobs.

## Mixing: balancing the parts

Mixing is where a pile of individual tracks — drums, bass, guitars, vocals, synths — becomes one
cohesive song. It's the bulk of the creative technical work.

- Setting levels so nothing buries anything else
- Panning and space so the arrangement feels wide and clear
- EQ, compression, and effects to shape each element's tone
- Automation so the song breathes and builds

You hand a mixing engineer your raw multitracks (stems), and you get back a single stereo
mix that sounds intentional and balanced.

## Mastering: the final polish

Mastering starts *after* the mix is done. It treats the finished stereo file as one thing and gets
it release-ready.

- Competitive, consistent loudness across platforms
- Final tonal balance — small moves, heard across the whole mix
- Cohesion so every track on an EP or album sits together
- Correct final files for streaming, vinyl, or physical release

If you want the longer version of what that final step does, I wrote about it in
[what mastering actually does](/blog/what-mastering-actually-does).

## Do you need both?

Usually, yes — they solve different problems. A great master can't rescue an unbalanced mix, and a
