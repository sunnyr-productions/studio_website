If you're looking for someone to work on your track, you've probably seen "mixing" and "mastering"
listed side by side and wondered whether you need one or both. They're two different jobs.

## Mixing: balancing the parts

Mixing turns a pile of separate tracks (drums, bass, guitars, vocals, synths) into one song. It's
most of the work.

- Setting levels so nothing buries anything else
- Panning and space so the arrangement feels wide and clear
- EQ, compression, and effects to shape each element's tone
- Automation so the song moves and builds

You hand over your raw tracks, and you get back a single stereo mix.

## Mastering: the last step

Mastering starts after the mix is done. It treats the finished stereo file as one thing and gets
it ready to release.

- Loudness that holds up next to other releases
- Small tone adjustments across the whole mix
- Making the tracks on an EP or album sit together
- Correct final files for streaming, vinyl, or physical release

If you want the longer version of what that final step does, I wrote about it in
[what mastering actually does](/blog/what-mastering-actually-does).
