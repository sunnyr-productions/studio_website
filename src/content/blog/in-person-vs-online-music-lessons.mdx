Both can work. It mostly depends on your instrument and how you like to learn.

## When online works

Voice, production and audio engineering all work well over video. For production, the screen can
even help: I can share mine to walk through a DAW, mark up a session, or pull up a reference track.

- No commute, so it's easier to keep going every week
- - Recordings of the lesson are easy to keep and revisit

## When in person is better

Guitar usually goes better in the room. Early technique (hand position, fretting pressure, how you
hold a pick) is easier to see and correct there.

- No latency, so playing *together* works
- Good for total beginners

For in-person lessons, I teach out of my sound-treated home studio in Corvallis, OR. If you're
local and learning guitar, that's usually where I'd point you first.

## If you're still not sure

If you're a beginner on guitar, come in person. For voice, production or engineering, online is
fine. Either way, we can start with a $20 half-hour [trial lesson](/lessons), and you can switch later.
