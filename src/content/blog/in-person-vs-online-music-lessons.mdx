One of the first questions people ask me is whether they should learn in person or over video. The
honest answer is that both work well — I teach both ways every week. What matters is matching the
format to your instrument and how you like to learn, not picking a "better" option.

## Where online shines

Voice, production, and audio engineering translate beautifully to video. For those, a screen is
often an *advantage*: I can share my screen to walk through a DAW, mark up a session, or pull up a
reference track in real time.

- No commute — easier to keep a consistent weekly rhythm
- Screen sharing makes production and mixing lessons genuinely hands-on
- Recordings of the lesson are easy to keep and revisit

## Where in-person wins

Guitar tends to go better face to face. So much of early technique — hand position, fretting
pressure, how you hold a pick — is easier to see and correct in the same room.

- Hands-on instruments (especially guitar) are easier to teach directly
- No latency, so playing *together* actually works
- Great for total beginners who want close feedback on the fundamentals

For in-person lessons, I teach out of my sound-treated home studio in Corvallis, OR. If you're
local and learning guitar, that's usually where I'd point you first.

## A simple rule of thumb

If you're learning **guitar** as a beginner, lean in-person. If you're working on **voice,
production, or engineering**, online is often just as good or better. And if you're not sure, we
can start with a $20 half-hour [trial lesson](/lessons) and feel it out — switching later is easy.

Wherever you're planted, there's room to grow. If you want to talk through what would fit you best,
just [get in touch](/contact).
