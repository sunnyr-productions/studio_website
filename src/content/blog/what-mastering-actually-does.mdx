---
title: "What Mastering Actually Does (and Doesn't)"
date: "2026-04-03"
excerpt: "Clearing up a step of the process that's easy to misunderstand."
---

People tend to think mastering is either magic or unnecessary. It's a final pass that does a few
specific things.

## What it does

- Brings your track up to a loudness that holds up next to other releases
- Smooths tonal issues that are easier to hear across a full mix than in isolation
- Makes the tracks on a release sit together
- Prepares final files correctly for streaming platforms, vinyl, or physical release

## What it doesn't do

- Fix a fundamentally unbalanced mix. That's still a mixing problem
- Replace missing elements or performances
- Turn a rough demo into a polished single on its own

It only helps once the mix is already solid. If you're not sure yours is ready, a
[consultation](/studio/consultation) first can save you money.
