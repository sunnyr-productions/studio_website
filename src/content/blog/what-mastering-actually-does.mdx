"Mastering" gets thrown around a lot, and it's often misunderstood as either magic or
unnecessary. It's neither — it's a focused, final pass that does a few specific things well.

## What it does

- Brings your track to a competitive, consistent loudness
- Smooths tonal issues that are easier to hear across a full mix than in isolation
- Makes sure a release sounds cohesive from track to track
- Prepares final files correctly for streaming platforms, vinyl, or physical release

## What it doesn't do

- Fix a fundamentally unbalanced mix — that's still a mixing problem
- Replace missing elements or performances
- Turn a rough demo into a polished single on its own

Think of mastering as the last 10% — meaningful, but only once the other 90% is already solid.
If you're not sure whether your track is ready, a quick [consultation](/services) beforehand can
save time and money.
