---
title: "Nobody Listens to Your EQ Curve"
date: "2026-11-01"
excerpt: "A lot of mixing advice is about how things look, because that's what works on YouTube. I fell for it too."
---

Most of the mixing tutorials we learn from now are videos, so a lot of what they teach is visual.
Look at this meter. Look at this signal. Here's what a good vocal EQ curve looks like. Here's the shape
your mix's frequency spectrum should have.

I'm not saying visual tools are bad. I still use them sometimes. But I used to rely on them far too
much. I'd check whether my mix looked like it had the right shape, or whether the low end had the
right bump, or whether the curve on my vocal looked like what a vocal EQ curve is supposed to look
like.

None of that is the question. The question is whether the frequencies are balanced in the right
relationship to each other in this song, and you answer that by listening.

## Formulas are easy to sell

A curve fits nicely in a thumbnail, and a signal chain is easy to sell. But every vocal is different,
and using the same chain on all of them takes the case-by-case judgment out of the job. It's an easy
trap to fall into. I fell into it.

The right mix isn't always the correct mix.

## What I'd try instead

Learn one pair of monitors or headphones really well. Listen to a lot of music you love on them,
until you know what good sounds like there. Then, next time you reach for a meter, make your decision
first with your ears, and check the meter afterward to see what it says.

It won't always agree with you. That's fine. People are going to hear your song, and nobody's going to
look at it.
