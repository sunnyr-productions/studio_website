---
title: "Stop Mixing With Your Eyes"
date: "2026-11-01"
excerpt: "Video tutorials taught a lot of us to mix by looking at the screen. I did it for years."
---

Most of us learn mixing from videos, and video rewards whatever looks good on screen. So we get taught
to watch meters and analyzers, and to copy the shape of someone else’s vocal EQ.

I did that for a long time. I’d pull up a spectrum analyzer and ask whether my mix had the proper
slope, or whether the low end had the proper bump. I’d look at the curve on a vocal and compare it to
the curve I’d seen in a tutorial. At no point was I asking whether the bass and the kick were getting
along in this particular song.

Formulas are appealing because they’re easy to teach and easy to sell. The trouble is that singers
aren’t interchangeable. A signal chain that flatters one voice can do nothing for the next, and if
every vocal gets the same treatment, you’ve stopped making decisions.

Analyzers still have their uses, and I still open them sometimes. I just try to open them last. Get to
know one set of speakers or headphones by playing music you love through them, then make your next EQ
move with your eyes off the screen. Check the display afterward if you’re curious. If it disagrees
with what you heard, your listeners won’t be looking at it anyway.
