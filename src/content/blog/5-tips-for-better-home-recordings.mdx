title: "5 Tips for Better Home Recordings"
date: "2026-05-12"
excerpt: "Small changes that make a big difference before you even touch a plugin."
---

A few habits will help your recordings more than any compressor or EQ plugin.

## 1. Treat the room, not just the mic

Reflections off hard surfaces are the most common problem. Hang blankets,
record in a closet full of clothes, or add basic acoustic panels. Anything that breaks up flat,
parallel walls helps.

## 2. Get gain staging right at the source

If your input is too hot or too quiet, plugins won't fully fix it later.
Aim for peaks around -12dB to -6dB with headroom to spare.

## 3. Use a pop filter (or a sock)

Plosives ruin good vocal takes. A cheap pop filter, or a sock stretched over a coat
hanger, solves this for a few dollars.

## 4. Record a few extra seconds of room tone

Thirty seconds of silence in the room, recorded at the same gain, gives you something to work
with for noise reduction when you mix.

## 5. Reference against a song you love

Play a professionally mixed track you love before and after your session. It resets your ears
and shows you where your recording stands.
