title: "5 Tips for Better Home Recordings"
date: "2026-05-12"
excerpt: "Small changes that make a big difference before you even touch a plugin."
---

Recording at home doesn't mean your tracks have to sound like it. A few small habits go a long
way before you ever open a compressor or EQ plugin.

## 1. Treat the room, not just the mic

Reflections off hard surfaces are the biggest culprit in amateur recordings. Hang blankets,
record in a closet full of clothes, or add basic acoustic panels — anything that breaks up flat,
parallel walls helps.

## 2. Get gain staging right at the source

If your input is too hot or too quiet, no amount of plugin processing will fully fix it later.
Aim for peaks around -12dB to -6dB with headroom to spare.

## 3. Use a pop filter (or a sock)

Plosives ruin otherwise great vocal takes. A cheap pop filter — or a sock stretched over a coat
hanger — solves this for a few dollars.

## 4. Record a few extra seconds of room tone

Thirty seconds of silence in the room, recorded at the same gain, is invaluable for noise
reduction during mixing.

## 5. Reference against a song you love

Play a professionally mixed track you love before and after your session. It resets your ears
and gives you an honest sense of where your recording actually stands.
