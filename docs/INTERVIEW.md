# Interview for the site copy

This is everything I need from you to write the site in your voice. Nearly all of it has one job: replacing sentences I had to write without knowing you.

## How to do this

- **Talk, don't write.** Use a voice memo or dictation and paste me the raw transcript. Don't clean it up. The "ums," tangents and half-finished thoughts show me how you actually talk.
- **Do it in chunks.** Each part takes 10 to 20 minutes, or about 2 hours in total. You don't have to do it all at once or in order. Parts 2, 3 and 5 matter most.
- **Each question has two pieces.** "What I need" tells you why I'm asking and where the answer goes. "Yap" is your prompt to just go. Answer the prompt however you like, and if you wander off topic, keep going. The wandering is often the best part.
- **Specifics beat summaries.** "A guy came in who could only play one Green Day song" is worth more than "I teach all levels." Small, odd or slightly embarrassing details are what make it sound human.
- **Skip anything.** Part 3 especially: share only what you'd be happy to see on a public website, and say "off the record" for anything that's just background for me.
- **Other people:** use first names or "a student" for now, and tell me if something needs permission before it can go on the site. I won't publish a story about a real person without you confirming it's okay.

## Part 1: Warm-up

**1. What you do**
- **What I need:** how you naturally describe your work. This shapes the homepage and the first line people read.
- **Yap:** You're at a party and someone asks what you do. Tell me the answer you actually give. Then tell me the longer version you'd give if they seemed interested. Do you say "teacher," "engineer," "musician," or something else?

**2. The name**
- **What I need:** where "sunny\*r" came from, what the asterisk means, how you say it, and how to spell it in plain text (roadmap 4.3). This could be a great About story.
- **Yap:** Tell me the story of the name and the logo. Who drew it, how the ambigram happened, what you were thinking, and whether anyone ever gets it right the first time.

**3. The motto**
- **What I need:** whether "Good things grow on the sunny\*r side" still feels like you, or just sounds like a slogan.
- **Yap:** Do you say that line out loud in real life? Where did it come from? What does "sunny" mean to you when it comes to music or teaching?

## Part 2: Your story

**4. Early music**
- **What I need:** real scenes for the About page. I only have "singing before full sentences" and a few year counts.
- **Yap:** What's your earliest memory of music? Who was playing or singing in your house? When did you first pick up a guitar, what was it, and what's the first thing you learned to play badly?

**5. Your teachers**
- **What I need:** the site says your music teachers stuck with you most, but not who they were or what they did. This is the root of how you teach.
- **Yap:** Tell me about the teacher who mattered most. What did they do that nobody else did? One moment you still remember? Now the opposite: did you ever have a teacher or a lesson that went badly? What did that feel like?

**6. On the Rocks**
- **What I need:** this is your strongest credential, and right now it's only a job title. I need to know what you actually did.
- **Yap:** How did you end up Music Director? What was a rehearsal like? Tell me about a singer you helped, a performance that went great, and one that fell apart. What did you learn about coaching people that you still use?

**7. The wedding band**
- **What I need:** a human, funny, lived-in detail. Almost nobody's site has this.
- **Yap:** Tell me your best wedding band story. What was the worst song you had to sing a hundred times? What does that job teach you about music that school doesn't?

**8. Becoming an engineer**
- **What I need:** how you got from singing to engineering, and how it connects to the studio side.
- **Yap:** When did you first record something? What did it sound like? When did you realize you cared about how things sound as well as playing them? How did podcast audio become your day job, and what does that job teach your ears?

**9. Your own music**
- **What I need:** whether you make your own music, what it sounds like, and whether it can go on the site. It could also solve the empty Portfolio.
- **Yap:** What do you make when nobody's paying you? What are you working on right now? What are you proud of? Is there anything you'd let me link to?

## Part 3: Neurodivergence and how you teach

*Share only what you want public. Your own words matter most here, and this page is the one I most can't write without you.*

**10. Your experience**
- **What I need:** the "Why I teach this way" story. How you describe yourself (ADHD, autistic, just "neurodivergent," or something else) and how specific you want to be in public.
- **Yap:** What was learning like for you as a kid? What was hard about it that other people didn't seem to struggle with? What's a time a lesson or a class just didn't work for you, and what would have helped? What word do you use for yourself, and how do you feel about the word on a website?

**11. What you actually do differently**
- **What I need:** concrete behavior, so I don't make things up. I removed my guesses about "predictable structure" and "breaks" because I didn't know if they were true.
- **Yap:** Walk me through what you do differently from a typical lesson. When you see someone start to shut down or get overwhelmed, what do you notice, and what do you do? Do you plan lessons or wing them? How do you handle someone who didn't practice?

**12. A student moment**
- **What I need:** one real example, anonymized if needed. It's the most convincing thing this page could have.
- **Yap:** Tell me about a time something finally clicked for someone after you changed your approach. What wasn't working, what did you try, and what happened? (Change any details you want. Tell me if this hasn't really happened yet, and we'll talk about what's true instead.)

**13. Parents**
- **What I need:** what you'd say to a parent, who's often the real reader of this page.
- **Yap:** Imagine a parent says, "My kid has tried lessons twice and quit both times." What do you tell them? What do you want them to know? What won't you promise? Can they sit in on the lesson?

## Part 4: The lessons

**14. Your real first lesson**
- **What I need:** the truth. My "first lesson" sections on every lesson page are guesses, and they're the weakest writing on the site.
- **Yap:** Pretend I just walked in for a trial. Walk me through the whole half-hour, from me knocking on the door. What do you say first? Where do I sit? What do we play? What do I leave with?

**15. Who you've taught**
- **What I need:** an honest picture, so I don't imply anything untrue about your students (the rule from roadmap 4.6). How many, what ages, and doing what.
- **Yap:** Who have you actually taught, formally or not? Who do you love teaching? Who's hardest? Is there anyone you'd rather not teach? What's the youngest age you're comfortable with, and why?

**16. Guitar**
- **What I need:** what's specific about how you teach guitar, for its page.
- **Yap:** What do beginners get wrong, and what do you tell them? What do you start people on? What do you think guitar teachers usually do badly? What song do you always end up teaching?

**17. Voice**
- **What I need:** the same for voice. People are often scared of this one.
- **Yap:** How do you deal with someone who's embarrassed to sing in front of you? What do you think most people misunderstand about their own voice? Tell me what actually happens in the first ten minutes.

**18. Production**
- **What I need:** which DAWs you can really teach. The site says Ableton, Logic and Pro Tools, but your gear list only names Logic. I need to fix whichever is wrong.
- **Yap:** How do you help someone with a folder of unfinished songs? Why do people get stuck? What's your own process like when you write something? Be straight with me about how well you know each DAW.

**19. Audio engineering**
- **What I need:** what a student would actually learn, and from what level.
- **Yap:** What's the first thing you'd fix in someone's home setup? What gear do people waste money on? What's the most useful thing your day job has taught you that you'd pass on to a home recordist?

**20. Practice and life**
- **What I need:** your actual attitude, which is probably more reassuring than my "fits into your life."
- **Yap:** What do you actually expect between lessons? What do you say when someone shows up having done nothing? Tell me about your own practice habits, honestly.

## Part 5: The studio

**21. How you mix**
- **What I need:** your real process and opinions for the mixing page and the blog.
- **Yap:** You've just received someone's tracks. Walk me through what you do from opening the session to sending the first version. What do you listen for first? What do you always do? What are your opinions that other engineers would argue with?

**22. A mix that fought you**
- **What I need:** a story. This is a better proof point than any list of features.
- **Yap:** Tell me about a mix that fought you. What was wrong, what did you try, what finally worked? Bonus: one you got wrong the first time.

**23. Who you've worked with**
- **What I need:** an honest idea of your studio experience, and what you can show or mention.
- **Yap:** Whose music have you mixed or recorded? Which projects are you proud of? What can I mention by name? What kind of artist do you want more of, and what kind of project would you say no to?

**24. Working with artists**
- **What I need:** what it's like to hire you.
- **Yap:** How do you handle notes you disagree with? What makes a client great to work with? What's the one thing you wish people would do before sending you tracks?

**25. Mastering and recording**
- **What I need:** anything specific to those pages, including what recording in your room is actually like.
- **Yap:** What do you tell someone who thinks mastering will fix their mix? What kinds of sessions work well in your room, and what doesn't fit? (A full drum kit? A band? Neighbors?) What's a recording session with you like, hour by hour?

**26. The room and gear**
- **What I need:** the human version of the gear list. "Nothing fancy, but I know every inch of it" is one of the best lines on the site, and I want more of those.
- **Yap:** Tell me how you treated the room yourself. What went wrong? Which instrument would you save in a fire? What's the story with the banjitar? What do you use all the time that nobody would guess?

## Part 6: Opinions and blog ideas

**27. What you'd argue about**
- **What I need:** points of view. The current blog posts could have been written by anyone.
- **Yap:** What advice in music do you think is wrong? What do YouTube tutorials get wrong? What makes you roll your eyes? Rant freely.

**28. Questions you get**
- **What I need:** the real FAQ, as opposed to my guess at it, plus blog topics.
- **Yap:** What do people actually ask you, again and again? What did you last explain to someone that they found useful? What do you wish more people knew?

**29. Corvallis**
- **What I need:** local detail that shows you really live there. It's good for people and for search.
- **Yap:** What's the music scene like in Corvallis? Where do you go to hear music or buy strings? What's Southtown like? Why are you there? Would OSU students be a good fit, or not really?

## Part 7: Your voice

**30. Words you'd never say**
- **What I need:** guardrails for my writing.
- **Yap:** Go look at the site and read a few paragraphs aloud. Where do you cringe? What would you never say? What do you say a lot that I should use? Do you swear? Are you funny or dry or earnest? Send me a text or email you wrote to a student or client, if you have one you can share.

**31. Sites you like**
- **What I need:** references for tone and design from real people.
- **Yap:** Whose website, Instagram or newsletter sounds like an actual person to you? Which music teacher or engineer sites do you like or hate, and why?
